Weighted demand in catchment
Sum of demand point weights within 3km. The core numerator of opportunity. Uses Haversine distance from candidate lat/lng to each demand point. — Why it matters: Directly measures how many customers are reachable.
D = ? weight_i for all i where haversine(cand, D_i) ? 3km
High impact
Source: demand_points
Group: Demand signals                                                                                                                             Demand point count in catchment
Raw count of distinct demand nodes inside 3km. Complements weighted sum — a zone with 20 small demand points is more spatially distributed than 1 large one. — Why it matters: Captures spatial spread, not just total weight.
count(D_i | dist ? 3km)

Source: demand_points
Group: Demand signals                                                                                                                                   Demand density (weight per km²)
Weighted demand divided by catchment area. Penalizes sparse zones even if total weight is high — a dense cluster is easier to serve than diffuse demand. — Why it matters: Efficiency of serving the area matters as much as raw demand.
density = D_weighted / ?·r²

Distance from candidate location to the weighted centroid of all demand points in catchment. A candidate perfectly centered on demand is better than one at the edge. — Why it matters: Reveals if the store placement is optimal within the demand cloud.
offset = haversine(cand, centroid(D_i·weight_i))

Variance of weights inside the catchment. High variance = one or two mega-demand nodes dominate; low variance = even spread. High variance is riskier. — Why it matters: Risk signal — uneven demand is fragile.

Group: Demand signals                                                                                                                             Competitor count in 3km
Number of competitor stores within the 3km catchment. The single most commonly used competition metric. Must be computed spatially — global count is meaningless. — Why it matters: Direct head-count of who you are fighting for the same customers.
comp_count = count(C_j | haversine(cand, C_j) ? 3km)

Group: Competition signals                                                                                                                           Competitor proximity decay score
Distance-weighted sum of competitors. Nearby rivals hurt more than distant ones. Uses inverse-distance weighting so a 200m rival scores worse than a 2km one. — Why it matters: Captures that competition is not binary — it is proportional to proximity.
comp_pressure = ? 1/dist(cand,C_j) for all j in 3km
